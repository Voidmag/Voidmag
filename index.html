Ben UFO:
The Unexpected

There is a specific kind of tension that builds in a room when Ben UFO is behind the decks. It’s not the anticipated drop of a familiar techno anthem, nor is it the steady, hypnotic groove of a microhouse set. It is the tension of the unknown.

As one third of Hessle Audio, alongside Pearson Sound and Pangaea, Ben Thomson has built a career entirely on the art of selection. In an era where production credits are often the prerequisite for a touring career, he remains an anomaly: a pure DJ, untethered by the need to play his own unreleased tracks to validate his presence.

"The dancefloor is not a place for certainty. It is a space for negotiation."

What makes his sets profoundly affecting is his refusal to adhere to a linear narrative. A typical two-hour slot might shatter the boundaries between UK garage, fractured bass, archival dubstep, propulsive techno, and abstract ambient textures. Yet, it never feels like pastiche.

Hessle Audio showcase, 2025.

The contemporary club landscape often leans heavily into easily digestible aesthetics. You know what you are going to get before you walk through the doors. Thomson disrupts this expectation. His methodology relies on a deep, archival understanding of dance music's lineage, allowing him to draw invisible lines connecting disparate genres.

This approach transforms the DJ set from a mere presentation of tracks into a live, unpredictable dialogue with the architecture of the room and the energy of the crowd. He forces the audience to listen actively, to lean into the discomfort of an unexpected rhythmic shift, and ultimately, to trust the selector.
